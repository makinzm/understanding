# Daily AI & Tech Trends - 2026-06-16

## Pick Up

1. [Running local models is good now](https://vickiboykis.com/2026/06/15/running-local-models-is-good-now/)
   - Why I recommend: Vicki Boykis argues that local LLM inference has reached a practical inflection point — a signal moment for ML engineers evaluating on-device vs. cloud tradeoffs in production systems.

2. [SpaceX to buy Cursor for $60B](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/spacex-buy-anysphere-60-billion-2026-06-16/)
   - Why I recommend: A $60B acquisition of the leading AI coding assistant by SpaceX reshapes the AI tooling landscape and raises major questions about the future ownership and accessibility of developer productivity tools.

3. [Qwen-Robot Suite: A Foundation Model Suite for Physical World Intelligence](https://qwen.ai/blog?id=qwen-robotsuite)
   - Why I recommend: A comprehensive foundation model suite targeting physical-world robotics from Alibaba's Qwen team — directly relevant to embodied AI and multi-modal learning research.

4. [GPT-NL: a sovereign language model for the Netherlands](https://www.tno.nl/en/digital/artificial-intelligence/gpt-nl/)
   - Why I recommend: Sovereign LLM initiatives are growing; this Dutch government-backed model illustrates the trend toward national AI infrastructure and raises interesting questions about training data governance and model alignment at scale.

5. [SubQ 1.1 Small](https://subq.ai/subq-1-1-small-technical-report)
   - Why I recommend: A small-model technical report — tracking the efficient-model frontier is essential for ML engineers choosing inference targets for edge and cost-constrained deployments.

6. [Miasma: Supply Chain Attack Targeting RedHat npm Packages](https://www.wiz.io/blog/miasma-supply-chain-attack-targeting-redhat-npm-packages)
   - Why I recommend: A detailed threat analysis of npm supply chain compromise targeting RedHat packages — critical reading for any team running ML pipelines that depend on open-source package ecosystems.

7. [State of Post Quantum Cryptography](https://www.wiz.io/blog/state-of-post-quantum-cryptography)
   - Why I recommend: With NIST PQC standards now finalized, this Wiz report gives a practical current-state assessment of enterprise PQC adoption — relevant for anyone designing secure ML inference APIs or data pipelines that need long-term confidentiality.

8. [Formal Methods and the Future of Programming](https://blog.janestreet.com/formal-methods-at-jane-street-index/)
   - Why I recommend: Jane Street's take on formal verification in production — provides concrete perspective on where proof-based correctness fits alongside ML-assisted development in high-assurance codebases.

9. [Stop Using JWTs](https://gist.github.com/samsch/0d1f3d3b4745d778f78b230cf6061452)
   - Why I recommend: A concise and well-argued critique of JWT misuse patterns; directly applicable to ML API authentication design where token-based auth is commonly (and often incorrectly) reached for.

10. [Is Meta destroying its engineering organization?](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/why-is-meta-destroying-its-engineering)
    - Why I recommend: The Pragmatic Engineer's analysis of Meta's engineering org changes is relevant for anyone tracking how large AI-first companies restructure for scale — and what that means for team productivity and technical leadership.
