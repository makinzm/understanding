# Hip-Hop: Basic Steps

## Overview

Hip-Hop here refers to the social/old-school street dance vocabulary that grew out of 1970s-80s African-American and Latino youth culture in the United States, danced socially and in freestyle circles (as distinct from the choreographed "hip-hop dance" seen in commercial videos, which borrows this vocabulary but adds set routines). A beginner with no prior dance training can learn these moves; they are commonly taught as the first lesson in hip-hop/old-school fundamentals classes, before any choreography.

## Key Concepts

### Up / Down (the foundation groove)

**Style/family:** Hip-Hop (foundational groove, danced to almost any hip-hop/funk/R&B beat with a clear backbeat).

**Body mechanics:** Stand with knees slightly bent and weight centered. The **Down** groove is a downward drive: bend the knees further on the beat, keeping the core tight, chest slightly forward, and let the bend absorb the beat rather than bounce off it. The **Up** groove is the recovery: from the bent position, lift the body slightly as you straighten the knees, landing the lift exactly on the beat (not before or after it). The two alternate: Down on one count, Up on the next, repeated in time with the music. Weight shifts side to side can be layered onto the same Down/Up timing once the vertical groove is solid.

**Why it matters:** Up/Down is the rhythmic base that almost every other hip-hop step (including Bounce, Rock steps, and Roger Rabbit below) is built on top of — a dancer who cannot hold a steady Down/Up groove will be off-time on everything else.

### Bounce

**Style/family:** Hip-Hop (foundational groove, a variant/extension of Up/Down).

**Body mechanics:** A continuous, small knee-driven bob: the knees bend and release repeatedly in place, letting the impact of each beat travel up through the legs into a slightly loose upper body (shoulders and head bob slightly, not rigid). The emphasis is absorbing the beat through the knees, not jumping off the ground.

### Rock step (back rock / front rock)

**Style/family:** Hip-Hop (foundational groove, layered on top of Down).

**Body mechanics:** From the Down groove, hinge the upper body backward from the hips while keeping the shoulders relaxed (back rock), or hinge forward using the chest and shoulders (front rock), each timed to land on the Down beat. Side-to-side rocks and a reset back to center can be added once the forward/back versions are steady.

### Roger Rabbit

**Style/family:** Hip-Hop (old-school social dance move, not a Locking move — see Comparison below).

**Provenance:** Sources attribute the name to the character in the 1988 film *Who Framed Roger Rabbit*, with the move becoming popular in hip-hop in the late 1980s; one source credits Bobby Brown's 1989 "Every Little Step" video as a key moment for its popularity. These accounts come from dance-blog/tutorial sites, not a verified primary account of the naming itself. **Sourcing note:** written tutorials disagree on the exact footwork — this document describes the most-detailed written version found (Steezy's), but at least two other distinct descriptions exist (see References), so treat the exact count/footwork as having real variation across teachers rather than one canonical standard.

**Body mechanics (Steezy version):** Start with a rock step, leaning the upper body back and then forward. As the tempo increases, rise onto the balls of the feet and hop while switching which leg is forward, producing the bouncing, rabbit-like travel that gives the move its name. The arms typically swing in opposition to the legs.

### Running Man (for comparison, not a Hip-Hop/Locking "basic step" covered in depth here)

**Style/family:** Hip-Hop (closely related old-school move).

Mentioned here only because it is frequently confused with Roger Rabbit (see Comparison).

## Comparison with Similar Moves

- **Roger Rabbit vs. Running Man**: Both are traveling, alternating-leg hip-hop moves with a hop/skip quality, and the Roger Rabbit is sometimes described as a *backward*-traveling variant of the Running Man's forward slide-and-step. They are historically and visually close enough that written sources sometimes describe one in terms of the other — this document treats them as related but distinct named moves, not synonyms.
- **Roger Rabbit vs. Skeeter Rabbit**: Despite the similar name, Skeeter Rabbit is a Locking move (see `dance/genre/Locking/basic-steps.md`), not a Hip-Hop move — the shared "Rabbit" naming is coincidental, not an indication that they come from the same style or share footwork.
- **Up/Down vs. Bounce**: Up/Down is a discrete two-phase groove (a clear Down, then a clear Up, timed to specific beats); Bounce is a continuous, smaller-amplitude repetition of the same basic knee action, used more for sustaining a feel between steps than for hitting a specific count.

## Applicability

These steps apply to anyone starting hip-hop/old-school social dance with no prior training — they are the first lessons in most beginner curricula (e.g. Steezy's and other online hip-hop fundamentals courses teach Up/Down and Bounce before any named move). They are danced socially, in freestyle circles ("cyphers"), and as a base for later choreography, typically to music with a clear, steady backbeat (funk, old-school hip-hop, R&B).
