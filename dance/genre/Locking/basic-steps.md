# Locking: Basic Steps

## Overview

Locking is a funk-era street dance style originated by Don "Campbellock" Campbell in Los Angeles in the late 1960s/early 1970s, and later performed by his crew The Lockers. It is one of the "four corners" of street dance alongside Breaking, Popping, and (later) Hip-Hop/Street Jazz-derived styles. Locking is largely improvisational/freestyle: dancers return to a signature freeze ("The Lock") between movements rather than following a fixed sequence, so the moves below are a shared vocabulary to draw from, not a fixed routine.

## Key Concepts

### Lock (the freeze that gives the style its name)

**Style/family:** Locking (foundational move; everything else in the style is built around returning to this freeze).

**Provenance:** Originated by Don Campbell, reportedly discovered when he could not smoothly perform the Funky Chicken and instead froze/locked each movement into a sharp stop — he then built a style around deliberately locking movement into freezes.

**Body mechanics:** From a loose, moving position, abruptly stop all motion into a sharp, held pose (commonly with one or both arms bent and a slight forward lean), hold the freeze for a clear beat or more, then release back into movement. The sharp stop (not the pose itself) is the defining mechanic — a soft or gradual stop is not a Lock.

### Points

**Style/family:** Locking.

**Body mechanics:** From a Lock freeze, extend one or both arms outward with the index finger (or whole hand) pointed, typically accenting a specific beat, sometimes combined with a handclap. Points are often layered directly onto a Lock rather than performed alone.

### Which-A-Way

**Style/family:** Locking.

**Provenance:** Attributed by one source to early Los Angeles locker Damita Jo Freeman. **Sourcing note:** written documentation of this move is thin — only one detailed source was found describing it, and it gives little step-by-step detail, so treat the description below as the best available summary rather than a fully verified standard.

**Body mechanics:** With the feet planted (no stepping), rotate the hips and torso smoothly from side to side, keeping the rotation concentrated in the hips/torso rather than the shoulders.

### Scooby Doo

**Style/family:** Locking.

**Provenance:** Credited by one source to Jimmy "Scoo B Doo" Foster. Name spelling varies across sources (Scooby Doo / Scoo B Doo).

**Body mechanics:** A "muscle man" Lock pose (arms bent and flexed, as if showing off biceps) followed by two separate kicks, with one hand pacing/swinging in time with the kicks.

### Skeeter Rabbit

**Style/family:** Locking — **not** the same move as Hip-Hop's "Roger Rabbit" (see Comparison).

**Provenance:** Credited to James "Skeeter Rabbit" Higgins as his signature move; one source attributes its creation instead to Anthony "Tony Go Go" Lewis, so the exact originator is not fully settled across sources.

**Body mechanics:** A kick-and-shuffle-hop performed to the side and/or front-and-back, sometimes finished with a Scoo B Doo-style kick-step.

## Comparison with Similar Moves

- **Skeeter Rabbit vs. Roger Rabbit**: Only the "Rabbit" naming is shared. Skeeter Rabbit is a Locking kick-and-shuffle-hop tied to a specific originator (James "Skeeter Rabbit" Higgins); Roger Rabbit is an unrelated Hip-Hop traveling move named after a cartoon character (see `dance/genre/HipHop/basic-steps.md`). They should not be treated as the same move or as variants of each other.
- **Lock vs. Points**: A Lock is the stop/freeze itself; Points are a hand/arm gesture frequently added on top of a Lock, not a separate freeze.

## Applicability

These moves apply to dancers learning Locking specifically, as distinct from general Hip-Hop fundamentals — a dancer who only knows the Hip-Hop Up/Down groove has not learned Locking's vocabulary. Locking is danced socially, in freestyle circles/battles, and as a distinct style within street dance performance, historically to funk music.
